  - Difficulty with stairs, long walking distances
  - Conditions like cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy, or limb loss
  - Support Includes:
  - Accessible classrooms/buildings
  - Alternative transportation access
  - Adjustable classroom furniture
  - Reduced course load or modified scheduling
  - Dexterity Impairments
  - Repetitive strain injuries (RSI)
  - Arm/hand injuries (e.g., broken limbs)
  - Ergonomic keyboards and writing aids
  - Note-taking assistance
  - Option to type exams instead of handwrite
  - Vision Impairments
  - Partial sight / low vision
  - Legal blindness / total blindness
  - Screen readers and magnification software
  - Large-print or Braille materials
  - Orientation and mobility assistance
  - Accessible electronic documents
  - Hearing Impairments
  - Partial hearing loss
  - Profound deafness
  - Real-time captioning (CART)
  - ASL interpretation
  - FM systems or amplified listening devices
  - Captioned videos and lectures
  - Chronic Physical Health Conditions
  - Multiple sclerosis
  - Cancer (during or after treatment)
  - Severe asthma
  - Chronic fatigue syndrome
  - Cardiovascular conditions
  - Flexible attendance
  - Deadline extensions
  - Rest breaks during class or exams
  - Medical withdrawal or retroactive drop options
  - Temporary Physical Disabilities
  - Post-surgery recovery
  - Sprains, fractures, or mobility loss
  - Temporary exam accommodations
  - Modified classroom seating
  - Access to note-taking services
  - Use of mobility devices

  - Lecture support: ASL, captioning upon request
  - Assistive tech: screen readers, adaptive software at library tech centre
  - Accessible campus: ramps, elevators, automatic doors, accessible parking
  - Academic advising: 1-on-1 support for accommodation planning
  - Student Services Hub: centralized accessibility coordination
  - Transition support: help with accessibility during university transition

[mental_accommodations]

  - Assistive tech (screen readers, speech-to-text)
  - Accessible formats (PDFs, audio)
  - Support for mental health, ADHD, etc.

  - Online support available

[documentation_requirements]
  - Certificate of Disability

[documentation_submission]
  - Complete online form.
  - Upload documentations at the end of the form.

[registration_process]
  - Activate UTORid and Uoft email
  - Obtain Certificate of Disbility Form from health care practitioner who is familiar with your disability, whose scope of practice includes your disability and who is licensed to make a diagnosis.
  - Complete Student Intake Form
  - After you submit a complete Student Intake Package, you will be contacted to schedule an appointment with an accessibility advisor.
  - The accessibility advisor will review your Student Intake Package and discuss any barriers or difficulties you have been experiencing in your academic progress to develop effective accommodations.
  - Make sure your UTORid is active and you have a UTmail+ account before your appointment.

[osap_eligibility]
  - Be an Ontario resident who is a Canadian citizen, permanent resident of Canada, or a protected person (international students are not eligible for OSAP)
  - Be registered in an OSAP approved program
  - Be enrolled in an eligible course load
  - Have previous OSAP loans in good standing, with no defaults or restrictions
  - Maintain satisfactory academic progress throughout your studies
  - Demonstrate financial need
  - Have not exceed the lifetime limit of student loan funding
